How it works

Three steps, no app to install and nothing to watch.

  1. Pick any market and your number

    Search any crypto, stock, ETF, commodity, future, index or FX pair — e.g. “email me if CommBank falls below A$161” or “if Gold goes above US$3,750/oz”.

  2. We check it every hour

    Prices come from public sources (CoinGecko, the ECB, Yahoo Finance). If a source fails we show it as stale — we never guess a number.

  3. You get an email when it crosses

    With the price, the time, and a receipt you can look up later. Every alert is also written to a public, tamper-evident log.

    Questions

    What is the difference between the tiers?

    Every tier — including Free — has full access to all markets across crypto, stocks, ETFs, commodities, futures, indices and FX, plus the public board, baskets, desk book and rule benchmarks. The only difference is how many trips you sign up to receive: 0 on Free (A$0), 4 on Tripwire (A$4.99/mo), 10 on Package (A$7.99/mo), or 25 on Gear (A$9.99/mo).

    Which markets can I select?

    Any public crypto, ASX/US/international stock, ETF, commodity, future (e.g. gold, silver, copper HG=F, crude oil CL=F, natural gas NG=F, S&P 500 ES=F or Nasdaq NQ=F futures), index or FX pair — search by name or ticker symbol on your account page or in the free board widget above.

    Is this financial advice?

    No. Tripwire tells you a price crossed a number you chose. It doesn't predict prices or tell you what to buy or sell.

    How fast will I hear about a cross?

    Prices are checked once an hour, so an alert normally arrives within about an hour of the cross. It's built for people who don't want to watch screens — not for day trading.

    How do I choose my markets and prices?

    After checkout you get a member key (shown once, not emailed). Open your account, paste it, then search and select any crypto, stock, commodity, future, index or FX pair — up to 4 trips on Tripwire, 10 on Package, or 25 on Gear — and set the price for each. Swap them whenever you like.

    How does the free trial work?

    Paid plans are free for 7 days, then billed monthly in AUD by Stripe. Cancel before day 7 in the billing portal and you aren't charged. Your card details go to Stripe, never to this site.

    Where do the prices come from?

    Public sources: CoinGecko for crypto, the European Central Bank (via Frankfurter) for exchange rates, and Yahoo Finance for global shares, ETFs, indices, metals, energy and futures. If a source doesn't answer, the board says “stale” rather than showing an old or made-up number.

    What does “tamper-evident log” mean?

    Each alert includes a fingerprint (a SHA-256 hash) of the alert before it. Editing or deleting any past alert would break every fingerprint after it, so the history can't be quietly rewritten. Anyone can check it in log.json.

    Can I cancel?

    Yes, any time, in the Stripe billing portal. No phone call, no email needed.

A leg is bought only while it is above its own 200-day average with positive 12-1 momentum; inside a cluster only the strongest momentum leg is held, so three 3x equity funds are one position and a choice — never three bets. The risk budget is split 1/vol across the clusters that are in, capped at 40% a leg, then the sleeve is scaled so its ex-ante vol is the tier's target — 15% core, 22% balanced, 30% high yield; a 20% drawdown halves the sleeve until equity is back within 6% of the peak.     Momentum — Own a leg while it is above its 200-day average with positive 12-1 momentum; size 1/vol, 40% cap. Wins in: long, one-way trends — 2020-21, 2023-24. Loses in: whipsaw: a market that crosses its average six times a year pays six round trips.

    Trend & breakout — Enter a leg above its 200-day and 50-day averages when within 2% (or 5 days) of a 63-day high, and hold while above both averages; equal weight. Wins in: breakouts that keep going, and quiet starts to big moves. Loses in: a market grinding sideways just under its high — the entry is always a little late and the exit a little early.

    Mean reversion — Own a leg while it is above its 200-day average but below its 20-day average (a pullback inside an uptrend), sized by depth of the dip over its own volatility. Wins in: choppy bull markets where every dip gets bought. Loses in: a real trend change: the 200-day average turns last, so the first three dips are the whole drawdown.

    Low volatility — Of the legs above their 200-day average, keep the calmest half by 63-day volatility; 1/vol inside that half. Wins in: risk-off years, and any client who measures the ride and not only the return. Loses in: a melt-up led by the loudest names — this style is structurally underweight what runs.

    Risk parity — Own every leg above its 200-day average, with no momentum filter at all, and split the risk 1/vol — one leg per cluster where the tier has clusters. Wins in: broad participation: the least opinionated way to be invested. Loses in: a market that is above its average and falling — it holds the leg the other four styles have already sold.
